The player inherits Uncle's workshop, Kettlewick Works. Villagers ask for a count of goods. The works do not play themselves.

## The shape of the game

Satisfactory is the reference, kept small. Code is the automation layer, and it comes after the player can do the job by hand: buttons on the machines, then one Run of a function, then Loop and `onTick`, then `on(event)`. The drone keeps a task after the function returns.

A new function unlocks when the player takes the quest that needs it. It is never bought. Run, Loop, and `on()` unlock when that quest is handed over. The shop sells conveniences only. Credits from quests are what the shop spends.

Veins are infinite. A poor vein spends 5 ticks for 1 rock, a normal vein spends 2 ticks for 1, and a rich vein spends 1 tick for 2. The early veins are poor. A normal vein sits between them and a later rich vein.

A quest is a quantity. It has no rate, no deadline, and no par time. There is no pause and no speed button. The clock keeps ticking, including while the tab is hidden.

The depot is where a quest is delivered. Goods do not leave the furnace for a villager on their own. A drone carries them to the depot, and the villager takes them from there.

There are no rooms. A world is one continuous map, about ten hours of play. New content ships as a new world. Credits and the works are not wiped to start it.

Build on the frame that is already on screen.
